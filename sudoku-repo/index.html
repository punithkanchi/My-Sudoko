Use pencil marks early. On Hard and above, jotting every candidate digit into empty cells (the Notes button) turns guesswork into visible logic.

Hunt for hidden singles. Sometimes a digit only fits one open cell in a block even though that cell still shows several candidates — check each missing digit, not just each empty cell.
5
Don't guess. If you're ever tempted to just try a number and see what happens, look again — every step in a proper Sudoku follows from what's already on the board.

Sudoku is one of the most enduring puzzle games around. The goal is simple to state: fill a 9×9 grid with digits so that every row, every column, and every 3×3 block ends up containing all nine numbers, 1 through 9. Despite that simplicity, it's a genuinely great brain workout — a logic puzzle that rewards focus and pattern-spotting rather than memorization. Play a little every day and most people notice their concentration and pattern-recognition sharpening over time. Pick a difficulty above and start your first grid — it won't take long before this becomes a regular habit.

About Sudoku

Sudoku traces its modern popularity back to Japan, though the number-placement concept behind it is older. It's a pure logic game — no arithmetic, no special math background needed. All it asks of you is attention and a bit of patience.

How to play Sudoku

Fill in the 9×9 grid so that every column, row, and 3×3 block contains the digits 1 through 9. The board starts with a handful of cells already filled in, and your job is to work out the rest using logic alone. A move breaks the puzzle if:

  • Any row ends up with more than one of the same digit
  • Any column ends up with more than one of the same digit
  • Any 3×3 block ends up with more than one of the same digit

Sudoku tips

Sudoku clicks quickly once a couple of habits become second nature. If you're just starting out, these two are worth practicing first:

  • Tip 1 — work the fullest lines first. Rows, columns, or 3×3 blocks that already have five or more numbers filled in are the fastest to finish. Go through their remaining empty cells and try the digits that haven't been used yet — often, one of them will only fit in a single spot once you account for what's already in its row, column, and block.
  • Tip 2 — think in bands of three. Mentally split the grid into three horizontal bands and three vertical bands of blocks. If two of the three blocks in a band already share the same digit, that digit must land in the one remaining block of that band — then check its nine cells individually to pin down exactly where.
